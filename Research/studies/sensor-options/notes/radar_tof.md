## Which XENSIV radar families and evaluation boards are relevant at short range?

### Takeaway
Infineon’s published IoT portfolio spans 24 GHz Doppler/FMCW and 60 GHz Doppler/FMCW. For a small box where range bins, angular information, and motion are useful, the 60 GHz BGT60TR13C is the best-documented development path here: its active demo board exposes raw radar data and the Radar Development Kit (RDK)/Radar Fusion GUI. Its published minimum detection range is 0.2 m, so a sensor/target closer than that is outside the stated range. The simpler autonomous BGT60LTR11AIP is motion-oriented, not a shape imager.

### Cited Findings
- Infineon lists 60 GHz Doppler products BGT60LTR11AIP, BGT60LTR11SAIP, BGT60LTR11BAIP; 60 GHz FMCW products BGT60UTR11AIP (1 Tx/1 Rx) and BGT60TR13C (1 Tx/3 Rx, angular information). Its current 24 GHz IoT page lists BGT24LTR11 (1 Tx/1 Rx) and BGT24LTR22 (2 Tx/2 Rx). — [Infineon 60 GHz radar portfolio](https://www.infineon.com/products/sensor/radar-sensors/radar-sensors-for-iot/60ghz-radar); [Infineon 24 GHz radar portfolio](https://www.infineon.com/products/sensor/radar-sensors/radar-sensors-for-iot/24ghz-radar)
- BGT60TR13C product details give 58–63.5 GHz, 1 Tx/3 Rx, 90° HPBW, 0.2 m minimum and 15 m maximum detection range, angle-of-arrival/range/speed capability, and an active/preferred status. Its description says the antenna-in-package is an L-shaped array and its FSM handles FMCW sweeps/acquisition. — [BGT60TR13C product page and datasheet link](https://www.infineon.com/part/BGT60TR13C)
- Infineon’s currently listed DEMO BGT60TR13C is an active kit comprising the BGT60TR13C shield and Radar Baseboard MCU7. The product page specifies 1 Tx/3 Rx, the 0.2–15 m product range, compatibility with RDK and Radar Fusion GUI, raw-data processing, and example presence, range/angle, segmentation, and seamless-tracking applications. — [DEMO BGT60TR13C](https://www.infineon.com/evaluation-board/DEMO-BGT60TR13C); [board user manual (UG091722)](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/30/44/infineon-ug091722-bgt60tr13c-shield-usermanual-en.pdf)
- The active KIT_CSK_BGT60TR13C is a connected-sensor development kit with BGT60TR13C, PSoC 6 MCU, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth, and an additional DPS368 pressure sensor. Infineon describes it as a compact, battery-capable IoT prototyping platform with user-configurable FMCW. — [KIT_CSK_BGT60TR13C](https://www.infineon.com/evaluation-board/KIT-CSK-BGT60TR13C); [kit user manual (UG105330)](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/30/44/infineon-ug105330-kit-csk-bgt60tr13c-xensiv-connected-sensor-kit-usermanual-en.pdf)
- Infineon lists 24 GHz DEMO SENSE2GOL PULSE (BGT24LTR11; pulsed Doppler motion/speed/direction) and DEMO DISTANCE2GOL (BGT24LTR11; software-controlled FMCW range measurement and 1-D human tracking). The portfolio page gives these as development kits; it does not establish that they resolve targets in a small docking enclosure or provide shape classification. — [24 GHz portfolio and kit descriptions](https://www.infineon.com/products/sensor/radar-sensors/radar-sensors-for-iot/24ghz-radar); [DEMO SENSE2GOL PULSE](https://www.infineon.com/evaluation-board/DEMO-SENSE2GOL-PULSE); [DEMO DISTANCE2GOL](https://www.infineon.com/evaluation-board/DEMO-DISTANCE2GOL)
- Infineon describes up to 5.6 GHz bandwidth for 60 GHz FMCW IoT sensors and up to 7 GHz available in the band; its product page separately says BGT60TR13C bandwidth is >5 GHz. Those are bandwidth statements, not a promise of a particular achieved range resolution in a given configuration. — [60 GHz portfolio](https://www.infineon.com/products/sensor/radar-sensors/radar-sensors-for-iot/60ghz-radar); [BGT60TR13C product page](https://www.infineon.com/part/BGT60TR13C)
- Infineon contrasts 24 GHz (lower free-space attenuation, externally designed antenna pattern, suitable for longer range) with 60 GHz AIP and higher-bandwidth short-range sensing. 24 GHz product/kit examples target range or 1-D tracking; multiple channels/antenna design are needed for angle/position. — [24/60 GHz comparison](https://www.infineon.com/products/sensor/radar-sensors/radar-sensors-for-iot)

### Inferences
- In a confined box, BGT60TR13C’s FMCW range data plus three Rx channels make it a more useful radar experiment than a single-channel Doppler sensor for mapping target movement/position. This does **not** make it a camera or a drone/hand classifier. The 0.2 m published minimum is important: mount/pose so the relevant return is beyond it, or validate near-range blind/poor-response behavior rather than assuming coverage down to the antenna.
- A radar sees moving reflecting parts and their range/Doppler structure, not “airflow” as a directly measured quantity. Rotor motion may create characteristic periodic/micro-Doppler signatures in flight tests, but a hair dryer’s motor/fan could also create motion returns if in view; external airflow can additionally move the box or objects. None of the cited Infineon pages documents classifier performance for this three-way distinction.
- 24 GHz can be useful for movement/range experiments and may tolerate a nonconductive enclosure better, but its wide-range orientation and customizable antenna pattern can complicate a tiny box. The 60 GHz FMCW demo’s range-angle/segmentation tooling is the stronger PoC candidate when a direct sensor-data evaluation is wanted.

### Gaps
- The source pages do not give BGT60TR13C range resolution under the demo’s actual configuration, near-field behavior below 20 cm, angular accuracy in this geometry, box multipath behavior, or a supported drone-versus-hand/airflow model. Determine these with the board and the intended box dimensions/materials.
- Product pages list other 60 GHz chips, but the reviewed sources did not establish equivalent current evaluation-board availability for each one. The 24 GHz pages identify the two development kits but do not document current stock or latest software support.

## What REAL3 ToF families/evaluation kits exist, and what are target, range, geometry, and optical constraints?

### Takeaway
The current public product records found for REAL3 include consumer/industrial IRS2975C (HQVGA, 240×180, 940 nm; bare die) and automotive IRS2877A (VGA system resolution, 640×480 upscaled, 940 nm; packaged). These are active product pages, not evidence of a complete ready-to-run camera module. Their product records identify companion VCSEL drivers. Publicly accessible evidence reviewed did not confirm a current Infineon standalone ToF evaluation kit or publish the complete lens, FoV, working-distance, ambient-light, or minimum/maximum-range limits needed for this box.

### Cited Findings
- Infineon marks IRS2975C active/preferred and describes it as REAL3 ToF for consumer/industrial applications: 240×180 pixels (HQVGA), 43 K pixels, 940 nm, bare die, 2.8 V rail, and companion VCSEL driver IRS9102C. The product page says it is designed for indoor and outdoor use and customizable use cases/output power. — [IRS2975C product page](https://www.infineon.com/part/IRS2975C)
- Infineon marks IRS2877A active/preferred and identifies it as automotive-qualified REAL3 ToF: 640×480 upscaled system resolution, 940 nm, PG-LFBGA-65 package, companion VCSEL driver IRS9103A, and on-chip background-illumination suppression (SBI) in each pixel. Its page claims performance in strong sunlight, but that wording alone is not a quantified ambient-light operating limit. — [IRS2877A product page](https://www.infineon.com/part/IRS2877A)
- The IRS2975C record identifies a bare die, whereas the IRS2877A record identifies a solderable optical BGA package; both list separate companion VCSEL-driver parts. — [IRS2975C](https://www.infineon.com/part/IRS2975C); [IRS2877A](https://www.infineon.com/part/IRS2877A)
- The currently accessible Infineon catalog pages/products reviewed identify these image sensors, but attempts to retrieve a current REAL3 ToF family landing page and likely generic ToF demo-board catalog URLs did not resolve. No current Infineon ToF kit can therefore be confidently named from this review. A third-party module or previously published/legacy kit should not be treated as a currently supported Infineon evaluation kit without checking its own status/documentation.

### Inferences
- ToF can independently deliver a depth image, so unlike a scalar proximity sensor it can support occupancy, outline/size, and motion-in-depth features. With fixed mounting and a calibrated, unobstructed view, a hand-versus-drone hypothesis is plausible from depth silhouette/extent and trajectory, but resolution, distance, occlusion, pose, and propeller blur affect separability. The sensor itself does not assign object classes.
- A 240×180 imager gives substantially coarser spatial evidence than a conventional RGB webcam; an 640×480 “upscaled” system resolution should not be mistaken for native independent depth samples without checking the datasheet’s pixel/readout details. For an enclosure, choose the field of view/lens to cover the entry and interior at the actual sensor-to-target distances; a wide field sacrifices pixels per target.
- Active near-IR illumination and receive optics imply line-of-sight and optical-window/lens requirements; opaque/dirty/scratched covers, sunlight or IR sources, highly absorptive surfaces, and reflective/multipath surfaces can affect ToF depth. This is general ToF engineering inference, not a measured IRS2975C/IRS2877A limit. A hair dryer’s airflow itself is not a ToF target; visible dryer hardware, the hand, and objects moved by air are.
- An evaluation setup likely needs the imager, lens, VCSEL/illumination driver and emitter, mechanical/thermal/eye-safety design, and capture software. For IRS2975C specifically, the bare-die package makes it less direct than a USB/CSI camera module. Confirm whether an integrator or kit provides these pieces before planning a bench test.

### Gaps
- No verified current Infineon ToF evaluation kit, kit manual, public complete sensor datasheet, or current SDK/module support was located in the accessible sources used here. Confirm directly with Infineon or a module supplier before treating the bare sensor product as a usable PoC camera.
- Needed but not established from the reviewed product records: FoV/lens options, native pixel architecture, frame rate, working-distance bounds, depth precision/noise versus distance and target reflectance, ambient-light rejection limits, cover-window compatibility, emitter power/eye-safety conditions, and calibration procedure. Obtain the current datasheets/application documentation and validate the optical stack experimentally.

## Could either technology alone distinguish a drone from a hand or external airflow, and what should be fused?

### Takeaway
Neither radar nor ToF alone is supported by the reviewed Infineon documentation as a reliable classifier for “drone in box” versus “hand enters” versus “external airflow, no drone.” They sense different evidence: radar range/motion/velocity/angular returns; ToF depth geometry and temporal change. Fuse those with independent airflow measurement and CapSense, and use the existing Logitech webcam as a visual reference/ground-truth channel where practical.

### Cited Findings
- Infineon describes radar functions including motion, speed, direction, range and (depending on antenna configuration) angle/position; its 60 GHz portfolio includes presence, people tracking, gesture, and FMCW object segmentation/tracking examples. The pages do not claim a drone-vs-hand classifier. — [24 GHz portfolio](https://www.infineon.com/products/sensor/radar-sensors/radar-sensors-for-iot/24ghz-radar); [60 GHz portfolio](https://www.infineon.com/products/sensor/radar-sensors/radar-sensors-for-iot/60ghz-radar)
- Infineon describes REAL3 as a ToF image sensor and gives the pixel resolutions and 940 nm wavelength for IRS2975C and IRS2877A; the pages do not document classification for the user’s three conditions. — [IRS2975C](https://www.infineon.com/part/IRS2975C); [IRS2877A](https://www.infineon.com/part/IRS2877A)

### Inferences
- Use spatial and temporal evidence jointly. A hand entering is likely a localized depth/reflectivity/range event with approach/retract trajectory; a drone has a larger persistent object volume plus motor/rotor periodicity and airflow localized around the craft; external airflow without a drone may change airflow readings but lacks an in-box drone-shaped depth/radar cluster. These are testable cues, not guaranteed invariants—hands can remain still, a drone can be stationary, props can be occluded, and an external fan/dryer can create mechanical radar returns.
- A useful fused decision should require corroboration: (1) ToF or camera occupancy/shape in the box, (2) radar movement/velocity/range signature or rotor micro-Doppler during flight, (3) one or more airflow/pressure channels measuring spatial or directional change, and (4) CapSense as a nearby hand/proximity/interaction cue rather than assumed object identity. Require persistence and clear state transitions to avoid one noisy channel declaring a drone.
- The webcam is valuable in the present manual-mock-up PoC as an inexpensive geometry/logging reference, but ordinary RGB video alone is sensitive to lighting and occlusion and has no direct depth. If camera-based detection is later used, evaluate it against radar/ToF and airflow ground truth rather than assuming any one modality can classify all edge cases.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer data establishes probability of detection, false-alarm rate, class accuracy, or robustness to rotor wash, hair dryers, hand occlusion, reflective box walls, or a stationary drone for any proposed sensor combination. These require experiments with representative enclosure, placement, and user scenarios.
- No specifications were provided for the candidate CapSense or airflow sensor/layout, so fusion thresholds, sample rates, or decision logic cannot be prescribed from the available evidence.

## Which technology is practical for the inert mock-up PoC versus later flight testing?

### Takeaway
For the current no-drone manual mock-up, start with the existing camera for labeled object-presence/trajectory recordings and use a BGT60TR13C demo as a radar data experiment if the test geometry stays beyond its specified 20 cm minimum. A fixed-view depth camera would be more directly informative about shape, but a current Infineon REAL3 kit/module path was not verified. Rotor-dependent discrimination must wait for actual powered/flight testing.

### Cited Findings
- The BGT60TR13C demo is an active board with RDK/Radar Fusion GUI support and range/angle/segmentation examples; its published product minimum detection range is 0.2 m. — [DEMO BGT60TR13C](https://www.infineon.com/evaluation-board/DEMO-BGT60TR13C); [BGT60TR13C product page](https://www.infineon.com/part/BGT60TR13C)
- The reviewed REAL3 records are component product pages (including bare-die IRS2975C), not a verified, current ready-to-use evaluation camera; the sources give image resolutions but omit system-level operating limits needed for an enclosure setup. — [IRS2975C](https://www.infineon.com/part/IRS2975C); [IRS2877A](https://www.infineon.com/part/IRS2877A)

### Inferences
- Inert mock-up PoC: test repeatable trajectories, occupancy and blockage with the camera; evaluate radar sensitivity to where/how the static mock-up is moved and to box clutter. ToF would be especially useful if a working camera/module is already available, because it yields depth outline without requiring a moving rotor signature. Do not use the mock-up to claim rotor-wash or in-flight classification.
- Later flight testing: add synchronized radar, depth/RGB video and airflow channels; capture approach, hover, landing, disarming, hand intrusion, and external blower/dryer trials as distinct labeled cases. Include no-object, stationary drone, moving hand, occluded craft and changing box configuration. Fit/validate a classifier on held-out trials and report false positives/negatives.
- The smallest number of distinct channels that can answer the user’s key ambiguity is likely airflow plus one spatial sensor: airflow helps distinguish an air source from an object; ToF or video provides shape, while radar adds motion/range and potentially rotor motion cues. CapSense can add an interaction/proximity cue but should not serve as the identity label.

### Gaps
- The correct sensor pose, target distance, box dimensions/material, mock-up material, rotor frequency/signature, airflow sensor placement, and RGB camera capabilities are unspecified. These affect practical results and cannot be inferred from vendor catalogue specifications.
- No exact performance or purchase recommendation is made: validate the chosen sensor(s) in the intended box and confirm supported current kits/documentation before procurement.
